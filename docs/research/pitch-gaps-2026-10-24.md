# Research note: the gaps in the 4V pitch script, 24 October 2026

Compiled on Saturday 24 October 2026 for [the pitch and poster plan](../lyceum-pitch-plan.md), from a research session with web research agents. Every page was retrieved on 24 October 2026. Quotations are as the agents read them, with typographic dashes shown as hyphens. Nothing in this note is registered in [claims.md](../claims.md) yet. Each row a slide or the poster uses gets a claims row first, after someone opens the source and checks the wording.

Keys, as in the other notes: **P** a primary document (a ministry, a regulator, a statistics office, an international body, a peer-reviewed paper, or a buyer's own board record), **S** reputable secondary (major press, an official statement reported by the press, a membership survey), **V** vendor or market-research material, said so beside the figure. **Derived** means arithmetic or a row count by the research session, not a number the source prints.

## 7. Competitors: what is occupied, and what is not

As far as public material read on 24 October 2026 shows, no product does all of this in one lecturer-facing flow: shows the exact job-posting sentences behind each skill count, carries that evidence into a recorded decision, checks the revised outline against it, builds an editable teaching pack from the approved outline, and reads results against the same outcomes. Every single step has an incumbent:

- **Programme-level labour-market data**: Lightcast Skillabi (a 0 to 100 programme-occupation score from programme text and "real-time job postings data"; quotations not shown on its pages), Coursedog Labor Market Insights ("Display real-time job demand, skills, and career outcomes directly within program proposals and records"; the source text not shown), Watermark with Lightcast data in programme review, Chmura JobsEQ, Gray DI.
- **Committee routing and audit trails**: Coursedog, Kuali, Modern Campus, Watermark, CourseLeaf.
- **Faculty teaching materials**: OpenAI's College Educator plugin for ChatGPT Edu (4 August 2026: "Faculty can update syllabi, create interactive websites or multimedia assessments, adapt materials for diverse learners, or package content for their LMS"; it can also use "student results"), Microsoft 365 Copilot Teach, Gemini for Education, Canvas IgniteAI Agent (it "uses Anthropic Claude and Model Context Protocol"), Blackboard's AI Design Assistant (included at no extra cost), D2L Lumi, Khanmigo for Teachers.
- **Outcome attainment**: Watermark, HelioCampus, Blackboard Outcomes, eLumen, Coursedog.

Nearest to joining the pieces: Coursedog, then Watermark. The defensible claim is the join and the sentence-level evidence trail, not "no one serves lecturers". The only artefact found that goes from postings to draft curriculum is CurriculumCatalyst, a research prototype with no human evaluation and no decision record (Springer, Discover Education, 10 September 2026).

The other Grand Final finalists are almost all learner-facing tutors (hackastone-and-competitors-2026-10-22.md, line 12).

## 8. Does AI in education skew to students?

Partly, and the evidence is thinner than the usual claim. By tool count (Reach Capital, 2023, over 280 tools) study and tutoring tools lead; no investor split of student-facing against educator-facing money was found (HolonIQ, GSV, Reach Capital, EdSurge). Better supported: students lead faculty in use ("Weekly GenAI use now spans 42% of students and 30% of instructors", Tyton 2025, V), and faculty report unclear guidance and too little training (Section 5). The safe sentence: most AI in education helps students study or helps teachers make materials; none we found keeps the evidence behind a course decision.

## 9. SerpApi, and licensed job data

- **The NVIDIA claim.** SerpApi's home page shows an "Nvidia" logo among fifteen under "They trust us" (raw HTML read 24 October 2026). AMD is not shown. No case study names either. A logo is the vendor's claim.
- **What it does.** "Our Google Jobs API allows you to scrape results from the Google Jobs search page."
- **Google's terms.** Google's Terms of Service (effective 30 July 2026) bar "using automated means to access content from any of our services in violation of the machine-readable instructions on our web pages (for example, robots.txt files ...)"; google.com/robots.txt carries `Disallow: /search` for all user agents.
- **Litigation.** Google LLC v. SerpApi LLC, N.D. Cal. No. 4:25-cv-10826-YGR, filed 19 December 2025 under the DMCA. On 20 July 2026 the court granted SerpApi's motion to dismiss, partly without and partly with leave to amend; Google amended on 10 August 2026; SerpApi moved to dismiss again on 25 August 2026; no ruling found by 24 October 2026. Reddit also sued SerpApi and others in October 2025.
- **The shield.** SerpApi's "U.S. Legal Shield" covers "claims brought under U.S. state or federal law in a U.S. court"; claims under foreign law fall outside it.
- **Licensed or official alternatives.** USAJOBS: "Public Data may be copied, stored, reformatted, adapted, analyzed, published, redistributed, and used for lawful commercial or noncommercial purposes." Adzuna: "for any use in a product or service that generates revenue or leads, a licence agreement is required". Lightcast: "A commercial license is required for for-profit or production use cases." Textkernel Jobfeed: "over 3 billion current and historical job postings" across 15 markets including APAC, priced from EUR 100 a month for its portal. Employers' own boards (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters): public, one employer each, terms per board.

## 10. NVIDIA's principles for trustworthy AI

Read on NVIDIA's own page on 24 October 2026 (<https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/trust-center/trustworthy-ai/>), **P** for what NVIDIA says it stands for:

- Privacy: "AI should comply with privacy laws and regulations, and meet societal norms for personal data and information privacy."
- Safety and Security: "Ensure that AI systems perform as intended and avoid unintended harm and malicious threats."
- Transparency: "Make AI technology understandable to people. Explain, in non-technical language, how an AI system arrived at its output."
- Nondiscrimination: "Minimize bias in our AI systems and give all groups an equal opportunity to benefit from AI."

The Grand Final's steering committee lists Simon See (NVIDIA) as a general chair (hackastone-and-competitors-2026-10-22.md, line 8); the judges are not named in the files.
