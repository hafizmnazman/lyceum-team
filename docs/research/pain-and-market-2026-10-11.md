## 2. Market research

### 2.1 Size and growth

No public estimate exists for "labour-market intelligence for education" as a segment. The nearest sized category is higher-education catalogue and curriculum management software: USD 3.46 billion (2024) to 7.62 billion (2032), 10.1% CAGR (Verified Market Research), or USD 3.48 billion (2025) to 8.99 billion (2032), 14.5% CAGR (360iResearch). Both are [estimate]; read them as low single-digit billions growing at roughly 10% to 15%. The data leader, Lightcast, is private and publishes no revenue; it ingests postings from more than 65,000 sources daily (UC3M description) [secondary].

### 2.3 What none of them do at the lecturer's desk

Judged on public product descriptions:

1. Collect on request for one course. Incumbents sell a standing feed bought centrally; the lecturer inherits a dashboard.
2. Keep the employer's exact sentence. Skillabi, JobsEQ and Journey normalise text into taxonomy skills; the quotation is gone before the meeting.
3. Show an honest count. Vendor deduplication and weighting cannot be inspected; Lyceum counts what it collected and says so.
4. Map to this course's outcomes and write the brief that an MQA 7.1.4 review or ABET Criterion 2 process needs.
5. Workflow tools (Modern Campus, Coursedog, Kuali, Watermark) route approvals and hold no external evidence; government portals aggregate nationally, not by course.

## 3. Market intelligence

In Lyceum's favour:

- Skills-first language is the norm in postings and surveys (WEF 39%, LinkedIn 70%), so employers write quotable requirements. Caveat: Burning Glass Institute and Harvard found real hiring change in "not even 1 in 700 hires", with 45% of firms changing in name only. Present counts as what employers ask for, not what they hire.
- Accreditors demand evidence: MQA 1.1.2, 6.1.6, 7.1.4; ABET Criterion 2, objectives consistent with "the needs of the program's various constituencies" through "a documented, systematically utilized, and effective process"; AACSB Standard 4.1, content that is "current, relevant, forward-looking, globally oriented".
- Micro-credentials multiply review events. MQA's Guidelines to Good Practices: Micro-credentials (2020) include market needs assessment; the OECD (2023) says evidence of their value "remains scarce", so each needs its own demand case.
- Governments push alignment: DOSM's closing statement, SkillsFuture's funding rule, TalentCorp's 620,000-job study.
- Lecturers already use AI weekly (30%, Tyton); a drafting assistant that keeps quotations fits the habit.

Threats:

- Data access. LinkedIn's User Agreement 8.2 (effective 3 Nov 2025) bars software used to "scrape or copy the Services, including profiles and other data"; LinkedIn sued Proxycurl in January 2025 and it shut by July. JobStreet's terms 7(d) (updated 20 July 2026) prohibit "data mining, robots, screen scraping, or similar automated data gathering" without written consent. Lyceum must collect through licensed APIs, employer-owned pages and user-supplied files; small on-request collection is the safe design.
- Incumbent AI. Lightcast's alignment score, Anthology's AI Design Assistant and Ellucian Journey generate alignment at scale. Lyceum's defence is provenance: quotations, honest counts, lecturer-level workflow.
- Signal noise. Postings overstate skills-based hiring (1 in 700).
- Procurement. Data is bought centrally (about USD 14,000 a year for one Lightcast seat), so a lecturer tool must be cheap to try or bought by a faculty.

## 4. What to say in a pitch

1. "In 2024 graduate unemployment in Malaysia fell to 3.2%, yet 1.60 million graduates, 32.2%, worked below their qualification, and under 25 the rate was 66%." (DOSM Graduates Statistics 2024)
2. "Employers across Asia Pacific report the mirror image: 77% cannot find the skills they need, up from 45% in 2014." (ManpowerGroup Talent Shortage 2025)
3. "The regulator already asks for this evidence: MQA's code requires 'market needs and graduate employability analyses' and a review system that 'must constructively engage stakeholders, including the alumni and employers'." (MQA COPPA 2nd edition, 6.1.6 and 7.1.4)
4. "Over 1,000 employers told the World Economic Forum that 39% of skills will change by 2030 and 63% call the skills gap their biggest barrier." (WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025)
5. "The OECD prescribes timely labour-market intelligence because skill demands move faster than policy cycles; Lyceum puts that intelligence on the lecturer's desk as exact employer quotations, counted honestly and mapped to course outcomes." (OECD Skills Outlook 2025)
