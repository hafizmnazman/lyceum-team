# Research note: HackaSTONE, readiness levels, pitch evidence, the market and the competitors

Date: 22 October 2026. For the Grand Final: what the competition is, how to state readiness honestly, what judges reward, who buys in universities, who else sells alignment, and what the education evidence allows us to claim. Compiled in a research session on 22 October 2026; sources marked vendor or secondary where they are.

## 1. What HackaSTONE is

- Official site hackastone.varteledu.com. Organiser: the Association of Global AI Technomics, Education and Exchange (GATEE). Theme "Agentic AI for Education": AI assistants for higher education, K-12, preschool and special needs, vocational and lifelong learning. https://hackastone.varteledu.com/
- Leadership: advisor Chris Dede (Harvard); general chairs Yiyu Cai (NTU), Simon See (NVIDIA), Sui Lin Goei (Windesheim); technical committee co-chairs Wouter van Joolingen (Utrecht) and Qi Cao (NUS). https://hackastone.varteledu.com/steering-committee/
- Scale and stages: over 50 universities, about 20 countries, over 150 teams; finalists 15 August; winners 29 October 2026 (ITS news, secondary). https://www.its.ac.id/?p=72149
- Grand Final: 29 October 2026, Amsterdam, inside "AI for Education The Netherlands 2026" (VU Amsterdam day one, UvA and HvA day two). Format: "max 8 min project presentation", "2 mins QnA", then "max 10 mins demonstration including QnA" at a team booth. Prizes: first S$1000 (up to 5 winners), second S$600 (up to 10), third S$350 (up to 15), merit S$200 (up to 20). Teams are strongly encouraged to bring university T-shirts and banners. https://hackastone.varteledu.com/grand-final/
- Published criteria, four, no weights on the site: Innovation and Novelty ("original thought, creative ingenuity, and a meaningful departure" from existing solutions); Technical Readiness Level ("maturity, robustness, and operational functionality of the implementation", "demonstrable performance"); Commercial Viability ("credible path to adoption"); Educational Significance and Applicability ("contribution to learning outcomes and its capacity for meaningful deployment"). https://hackastone.varteledu.com/spotlight/. The briefing slide photographed on 22 October shows Innovation 30%, Technology Readiness 20%, Commercial Viability 15%, Education Significance 15%, Presentation 10%, Demo 10%, marked "to be firmed"; confirm against the finalist pack.
- Finalists announced 12 to 14 October 2026 include EmoAgent (SCNU, social-emotional dialogue agent), Charlie humanoid tutor (SUSS), Signify (ITB, deaf learners), Cognitive Arena (UESTC), Cogniva (ITB, learning by teaching), EcoLearn (BVRIT) and EduVent (ITS). Nearly all are learner-facing tutors; a lecturer-facing decision tool is a positioning gap. https://hackastone.varteledu.com/news-update/
- Past winners: none found; this looks like the first edition.

## 2. Technology Readiness Level, adapted to software

- EU wording (Horizon 2020 Annex G, carried into Horizon Europe): TRL 5 "technology validated in relevant environment"; TRL 6 "technology demonstrated in relevant environment"; TRL 7 "system prototype demonstration in operational environment"; TRL 8 "system complete and qualified"; TRL 9 "actual system proven in operational environment". https://ec.europa.eu/research/participants/data/ref/h2020/wp/2014_2015/annexes/h2020-wp1415-annex-g-trl_en.pdf
- NASA ESTO software column: TRL 5 "End-to-end software elements implemented and interfaced with existing systems/simulations"; TRL 6 "Prototype implementations of the software demonstrated on full-scale realistic problems", partially integrated, limited documentation; TRL 7 "Prototype software exists having all key functionality available for demonstration and test", integrated with operational systems; TRL 8 fully debugged, documentation complete, verification done; TRL 9 run in the operational environment. https://esto.nasa.gov/trl/
- NASA reviewer checklist of weak TRL claims: "The 'relevant' environment is not defined" and "Software development is not addressed". Define the relevant environment: real postings, a real syllabus, a real lecturer. https://lws.larc.nasa.gov/vfmo/pdf_files/Common-causes-TRL-Weaknesses-Ver03.080118.pdf
- Honest placement for Lyceum: software TRL 6. TRL 7 needs one department running it for a term inside its LMS; TRL 8 needs full documentation and verification.
- EdTech evidence ladders: Digital Promise "Research-Based Design" (ESSA Tier 4) needs a logic model with five citations, an annotated bibliography of five design decisions and one qualitative user study; "Evidence-Based Edtech" (Tier 3) needs a well-designed quantitative study showing benefits for learners. https://digitalpromise.org/product-certifications/
- US Department of Education letter, 20 August 2026: judge edtech on outcomes, not usage; "Responsible design is the floor". https://www.edsurge.com/news/department-of-education-issues-long-awaited-edtech-guidance-for-states-and-districts. UK DfE EdTech Testbed, 24 September 2026: rapid evaluations then EEF trials, on outcomes, workload and inclusion. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/edtech-testbed-programme/edtech-testbed-programme-guidance

## 3. Pitch and demo evidence

- Y Combinator, Geoff Ralston (2016): intrigue, do not close; say what you do plainly; three or four "vertebrae"; one presenter; about seven words per slide; restate key points at the close. https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/
- Paul Graham: say what you do in the first sentence; "A demo explains what you've made more effectively than any verbal description"; never have one person talk while operating the computer; no more than four or five numbers; craft a memorable one-liner. https://paulgraham.com/investors.html
- Stripe Atlas, Patrick McKenzie: have a demo ready in 20 seconds; "Give people a link that drops them into a fully authenticated session on a pre-populated account"; fake slow third-party steps openly; know competitors. https://stripe.com/blog/atlas-pitching
- A hackathon judge (July 2026): demos that do not run are the largest score differentiator; opening with context wastes a third of the slot; admitting a weakness raised scores; judges ask "who is this for specifically" and "what breaks first at scale"; keep a 60-second screen recording; "Pick the sentence you want a judge to repeat to another judge an hour later." https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l
- Devpost: the video is one of the first things judges look at, usually under three minutes, elevator pitch first, show the product in use, no slide decks in the video. https://info.devpost.com/blog/6-tips-for-making-a-hackathon-demo-video. MLH: the video should be a demo, not a presentation. https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/judging-and-submissions/judging-plan.md
- Red Gate, 10 September 2026: across about 150 recorded sessions "the demos failed in 2/3 of the sessions"; record every demo and narrate the recording live; never debug live. https://www.red-gate.com/simple-talk/?p=111618

## 4. Commercial viability in university edtech

- Coursera 10-K (February 2026) cites "the complicated approval process within some of these entities" and "longer sales cycles" abroad. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1651562/000165156226000015/cour-20251231.htm. A vendor guide (Stackmatix, September 2026) puts higher-education cycles at 6 to 12 months, split between department-led purchases and central IT. https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/gtm-for-edtech-startups. No authoritative pilot-to-licence conversion benchmark was found.
- Who competitors sell to: Gray DI targets presidents, CFOs, provosts, deans and institutional research; Coursedog targets provosts, registrars and CIOs; Lightcast's quoted customer is a director of institutional research; Chmura's output is "the case you take to your board or your funder". Nobody sells to the lecturer.
- Pricing anchors (Vendr, secondary): Canvas "$3 to $12 per active user" a year, median contract $21,789. https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/instructure. Coursera for Business $250 to $550 per learner a year. https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/coursera-e79d6. SkillsEngine Team at $999 a month. https://skillsengine.com/. Blackboard's AI Design Assistant is "included as standard" at no extra cost, so AI course generation is already priced at zero inside the LMS. https://www.blackboard.com/ai-design-assistant
- Tool fatigue: WGU Labs faculty survey (2023): 77% feel "always on the job because of technology", 83% still say edtech enhances teaching, only 30% trust their institution to choose the right products, more than half fear edtech reduces autonomy over course design. https://wgulabs.org/posts/survey-higher-ed-faculty-mixed-on-edtech-and-ai-two-thirds-say-higher-ed-is-headed-in-wrong-direction
- Tyton Partners, Time for Class 2026 (June 2026, 3,000 or more respondents): 89% of institutions willing to invest more in faculty-facing AI; faculty daily AI use 25% against administrators 43%; 63% of faculty say they assign real-world projects but only 26% of students report completing one; 60% of students against 70% of administrators believe courses prepare for work; 12% of institutions embed career-connected learning across all undergraduate departments. https://tytonpartners.com/time-for-class-2026/
- EDUCAUSE Horizon Report 2026 (May 2026): leaders put "greater emphasis on alignment with the labor market"; "growing pressure to prove credential ROI"; staffing cuts worsening burnout; "Digital learning is becoming a sustainability strategy"; awareness of AI's environmental cost is growing. https://library.educause.edu/-/media/files/library/2026/5/2026hrteachinglearning.pdf

## 5. Competitors and adjacent products (vendor pages unless noted)

- Lightcast Skillabi (score launched 3 June 2025): program-occupation score 0 to 100 from course descriptions, top-10 missing skills, "18 billion labor market data points", quote-only. Not offered: lecturer-run collection, quoted postings, posting counts, teaching materials. https://lightcast.io/solutions/software/skillabi/align-courses
- Chmura JobsEQ Program Alignment: CIP-to-SOC alignment, "Over 350 million job postings from 45,000+ sites", board reporting, aggregates only. https://www.chmura.com/jobseq/education/program-alignment
- Coursedog: approval workflows plus "Labor Market Insights" inside program proposals; program level, source unnamed. https://www.coursedog.com/products/labor-market-insights
- Watermark Curriculum Strategy: catalogue and approval workflows; no labour-market data on the page. https://www.watermarkinsights.com/discover/lp-curriculum-strategy/
- Anthology Blackboard AI Design Assistant: modules, questions, rubrics and images from existing course context; no labour-market data, no citations. https://www.blackboard.com/ai-design-assistant
- Ellucian Journey (February 2026): labour-market intelligence for continuing-education programmes at 100 or more institutions; learner recommendations, not degree-course design. https://www.ellucian.com/newsroom/ellucian-journey-named-2025-campus-technology-product-year
- Instructure Canvas Career (29 January 2026): skills-first platform for adult and workforce learning. https://www.stocktitan.net/news/INST/instructure-advances-skills-first-workforce-learning-with-broad-w99l8u8l94ks.html
- Coursera for Campus, Credly by Pearson, Riipen, Gray DI, Jobspeaker, SkillsEngine: content, credentials, projects and programme analytics; none at the lecturer's desk.
- Academic signal: UNSW (INTED 2026) mapped CLOs to nine skills with machine learning plus educator review "over 90% accuracy across more than 1,000 courses"; taxonomy mapping, not quoted employer evidence. https://library.iated.org/view/CHUGH2026MAP
- Net gap: no product shows exact posting quotations with honest denominators, lets a lecturer run their own collection, or produces a meeting brief plus an editable teaching pack; all sell to institutional research, provosts or boards.

## 6. Educational significance

- Biggs, constructive alignment: "The key is that all components in the teaching system, the curriculum and its intended outcomes, the teaching methods used, the assessment tasks, are aligned to each other." https://www.biologie.uzh.ch/dam/jcr:94dbcc23-1c4d-4892-bab8-ba16abc4ef66/ConstructiveAlignment_Biggs.pdf
- Limits: Scandurra et al 2023, Studies in Higher Education, 87 papers: the employability-programme evidence base "is dominated by small-scale case studies and evaluations that are not sufficiently robust" to infer causal effects. https://www.uloyola.es/en/scientific-offer/publications/do-employability-programmes-in-higher-education-improve-skills-and-labour-market-outcomes-a-systematic-review-of-academic-literature-article
- Does labour-market information change decisions? Baker, Bettinger, Jacob and Marinescu (NBER 2017): students overestimated salaries by about 13% and underestimated employment probability by almost 25%. https://www.nber.org/papers/w23333. Kerr et al (IZA 2015, 97 Finnish schools): a third of students updated beliefs, but enrolment did not shift. https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/9372. Lesson: information shifts beliefs more than behaviour, so the evidence must arrive with a decision and a deliverable. No study of lecturer-targeted labour-market information changing course decisions was found: a pilot can fill it.
- Employer view, AAC&U Agility Imperative (December 2025, 1,030 employers): 85% say colleges are "doing a good job of preparing students for the workforce"; nine in ten say AI-related skills matter. https://www.aacu.org/research/the-agility-imperative

## Do this in the pitch and the demo

1. Innovation: open with the sentence a judge will repeat an hour later, then name what is new against Skillabi and JobsEQ in 30 seconds.
2. Innovation: put a receipt on screen in the first minute (exact quotation, posting count, date, source), because no competitor page claims this.
3. Technology Readiness: state "software TRL 6" in NASA wording, show the check and smoke suites as evidence, and name the TRL 7 step (one department, one term, inside its LMS).
4. Technology Readiness: demo from a pre-populated, signed-in workspace, mock slow third-party calls and say so, never debug live.
5. Technology Readiness: answer "what breaks first at scale" before they ask (posting coverage, representativeness, model drift); honest limits score.
6. Commercial Viability: anchor against free LMS generation (Blackboard), quote-only institutional data (Lightcast, Chmura) and Canvas at $3 to $12 per user, then propose a per-department subscription that rides existing LMS and data contracts.
7. Commercial Viability: show the demand signal (89% of institutions want to invest in faculty-facing AI; 30% faculty trust in institutional tool choice) and the "no new platform" integration slide; give the 6-to-12-month cycle and a department-led pilot path.
8. Education Significance: frame the teaching pack as constructive alignment: evidence to intended outcomes to aligned activities and assessment, and trace one quotation through all three.
9. Education Significance: be candid that employability evidence is small-scale and that information alone shifts beliefs more than behaviour, so Lyceum pairs evidence with a decision and a deliverable; propose a Tier 4 logic model plus lecturer study, then a Tier 3 before-and-after design.
10. Education Significance: use the perception gap (63% of faculty against 26% of students on real-world projects; 52% underemployed) rather than "universities are failing" (85% of employers say colleges do a good job).
11. Presentation: one presenter, slides as supporting cast, no more than five numbers, restate three points at the close, rehearse until smooth, and keep hard-question answers ready for the two-minute Q&A.
12. Demo: at the booth, split narrator and operator, run one end-to-end path, test on venue wifi with an offline mode, keep the narrated 60-second recording and the video as fallbacks, and bring the university banner the organisers ask for.

Unverified or missing: the official weights, the Presentation and Demo descriptors, any Instructure and Lightcast tie, MLH's criteria page text, IES ESSA tier text, pilot-to-licence conversion norms.
