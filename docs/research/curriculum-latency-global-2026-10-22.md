# Research note: curriculum latency as a global problem

Date: 22 October 2026. Written for the pitch and the landing page: the problem restated at world level, so that Lyceum is not read as a Malaysian fix. Every item was checked in a research session on 22 October 2026 against the page named; the key says how far.

Key: **P** primary document fetched and quoted; **S** reputable secondary; **V** vendor material; **U** seen only in a search snippet, page blocked, treat as unverified.

## 1. How long curriculum change takes

- "It generally requires twelve to eighteen months for new degree programs to complete the internal review and approval process" (Board of Governors, state endorsement, HLC may also be needed). Wayne State University, provost policy page. https://provost.wayne.edu/programs/approval-process **P**
- "From start to finish, the full approval process can take up to 18 months", including Board of Regents, THECB and SACSCOC. Texas Tech University, New Programs Timelines. https://www.depts.ttu.edu/provost/curriculum/new-programs/timelines.php **P**
- Program approvals "6 months to 2 years"; course changes quicker because no external approval. University of South Carolina. https://www.sc.edu/about/offices_and_divisions/advising/curriculum_services/approval_process/index.php **U**
- Course level, US: "the minimum time needed is approximately three months. It is not uncommon for the process to take four to six months." Indiana University College of Arts and Sciences. https://curriculum.college.indiana.edu/courses/courses-faq.html **P**
- Annual catalogue cycle: core-course changes approved at college level by 1 September pass the college committee in November and the university committee in December, then the state board, and take effect "the following academic year". University of North Texas. https://vpaa.unt.edu/ccg/timeline.html **P**
- UK: "a new programme must be approved by the Senate Portfolio Scrutiny Panel (SPSP) at least 12 months before it is implemented." Regent's University London, Academic Regulations 2018/19. https://www.regents.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2019-01/B-Regents-Quality%20Assurance%20&%20Enhancement%20Processes_18-19.pdf **P**
- UK: "a validation event should normally take place no later than 12 months prior to the new course commencing." University of Northampton. https://www.northampton.ac.uk/about-us/governance-and-management/management/quality-and-standards/validation/ **P**
- UK: planning "up to two years in advance of validation" for UCAS listing (Queen Margaret University); 24 months at Bangor; UCL deadlines 12 to 22 months before a September start. **U**
- Australia: the TEQSA Act gives nine months for initial course accreditation decisions and sets no limit for renewals; "renewal applications sometimes took longer because there was no legal minimum time limit". Government News, 2015, updated 2024. https://www.governmentnews.com.au/?p=21677 **S**
- Singapore: autonomous universities review curricula through academic advisory boards "every two to five years"; polytechnics and ITE annually at course level and institutionally "around every three years". MOE parliamentary reply, 12 September 2022. https://www.moe.gov.sg/news/parliamentary-replies/20220912-review-of-institutes-of-higher-learnings-curricula **P**
- The term "curriculum lag" is used in a peer-reviewed article: Ihrig, Peate and Wolla, "Curriculum lag challenges and strategies", Journal of Economic Education 55(2), 2024. https://doi.org/10.1080/00220485.2024.2304896 (metadata verified, text paywalled).
- Not found: a global measured study of approval-to-delivery lag; EU and India timeline figures.

## 2. Skill half-life and churn

- "On average, workers can expect that two-fifths (39%) of their existing skill sets will be transformed or become outdated over the 2025-2030 period" (44% in 2023, 57% in 2020); "63% of employers identifying [skill gaps] as a major barrier"; "AI and big data top the list of fastest-growing skills"; survey of over 1,000 employers, 14 million workers, 55 economies. WEF, Future of Jobs Report 2025. https://www3.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Future_of_Jobs_Report_2025.pdf **P**
- "By 2030, 70% of the skills used in most jobs will change, with AI emerging as a catalyst". LinkedIn, Work Change Report, January 2025. https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/economicgraph/en-us/PDF/Work-Change-Report.pdf **V** (the method behind 70% is not explained)
- "the half-life of a learned skill is estimated to be five years and even shorter for technical skills"; time to close a skills gap by training rose "from 3 days to 36 days" (2014 to 2018). IBM Institute for Business Value, 2019. https://ibm.com/downloads/cas/EPYMNBJA **V**
- "32% of the skills required for the average job are different in 2024 than they were in 2021"; the top quarter of occupations saw 75% of skills change; generative-AI postings up 15,625% in three years. Lightcast, The Speed of Skill Change, 22 January 2025. https://lightcast.io/resources/blog/skills-change-us-press-release **V**
- 37% of the top 20 skills for the average US job changed between 2016 and 2021 (15 million postings). Lightcast, Burning Glass Institute and BCG, May 2022. https://lightcast.io/resources/blog/new-report-measures-blazing-pace-of-skill-change **V**
- "Skills requirements are evolving 66% faster in AI-exposed jobs than in other roles"; AI wage premium 56% in 2024. PwC, 2025 Global AI Jobs Barometer. https://press.pwc.be/ai-drives-productivity-and-wage-growth-pwc-global-study-reveals **V**
- AI wage premium 62%; AI-skill jobs growing about eight times as fast as the overall market; over one billion job advertisements in 27 countries. PwC, 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer, 15 June 2026. https://kyodonewsprwire.jp/release/202606150856 **V**

## 3. Accreditation clauses that demand employer or market evidence

- ABET EAC 2025-2026, Criterion 2: objectives "consistent with the mission of the institution, the needs of the program's various constituencies" plus a documented process "involving program constituencies, for the periodic review of these program educational objectives"; Criterion 4: "The results of these evaluations must be systematically utilized as input for the program's continuous improvement actions." https://www.abet.org/accreditation/accreditation-criteria/criteria-for-accrediting-engineering-programs-2025-2026/ **P**
- AACSB 2026 Global Standards (continuous improvement visits from 2027/28): 4.1 "Curricula are continuously renewed to reflect current and emerging business theories and practices, industry evolution, and societal trends"; 4.2 "Curricula are reviewed on a planned and consistent review cycle"; 7.2 curriculum "current, relevant, forward-looking, globally oriented". https://www.aacsb.edu/educators/accreditation/business-accreditation/aacsb-business-accreditation-standards **P**
- AUN-QA Guide v4.0 (ASEAN), 1.4: "the requirements of the stakeholders, especially the external stakeholders, are gathered, and that these are reflected in the expected learning outcomes"; 2.7: curriculum "reviewed periodically and that it remains up-to-date and relevant to industry." Wording verified in two 2023 self-assessment reports reproducing v4.0. https://fet.iuh.edu.vn/upload/files/web_SAR_2023.pdf **S**
- UK QAA, External Expertise (November 2018): "Providers are encouraged to engage with employers and other external stakeholders at all stages of course design, approval and review in order to ensure that their courses continue to be relevant and fit for purpose in line with employer needs". https://www.qaa.ac.uk/docs/qaa/quality-code/advice-and-guidance-external-expertise.pdf **P**
- Australia HESF 2021, 3.1.2: content must engage with "emerging concepts that are informed by recent scholarship, current research findings and, where applicable, advances in practice" (verified in a provider policy reproducing the standard). https://aih.edu.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/ACA-HE-08-Course-Design-Policy-and-Procedure-2025.2.pdf **S**
- EU ESG 2015, 1.9: "Institutions should monitor and periodically review their programmes to ensure that they achieve the objectives set for them and respond to the needs of students and society"; the guideline lists "The changing needs of society" and "Programmes are reviewed and revised regularly involving students and other stakeholders." ENQA. https://www.enqa.eu/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/ESG_2015.pdf **P**
- India NAAC 1.1.1: curricula "have relevance to the local, national, regional and global developmental needs"; 1.4.1: structured feedback on the syllabus from students, teachers, employers and alumni. https://old.ncet.co.in/iqac/important-criteria **S**. NEP 2020: "greater industry-academic linkages", "Increase employability potential of higher education programmes". https://www.ugc.gov.in/pdfnews/5294663_Salient-Featuresofnep-Eng-merged.pdf **P**
- Singapore MOE, 8 April 2026: SkillsFuture Skills Frameworks ("38 sectors", "continually refreshed") are built from "labour market surveillance data, such as job posting data" with employers, associations, unions and agencies. https://www.moe.gov.sg/news/parliamentary-replies/20260408-industry-skills-councils-involvement-in-development-of-current-skills-framework-and-practitioner-engagement-to-ensure-alignment-with-operational-skills **P** (a government already treats job postings as evidence)
- Malaysia MQA COPPA 2nd edition, 6.1.6 and 7.1.4: see docs/research/pain-and-market-2026-10-11.md.

## 6. Labour-market data in curriculum review, and the criticism

- WGU and Lightcast pilot (2019): competencies mapped against "millions of job postings"; "almost 20,000 was whittled down to about 1,000 in-demand, marketable skills for each program". https://lightcast.io/resources/blog/western-governors-university-maps-curriculum-to-in-demand-skills **V** (no outcome metrics)
- Lightcast Skillabi, 3 June 2025: "Quantifies program relevance with a clear, objective score (0-100)"; "used by over 1,000 educational institutions". https://lightcast.io/resources/blog/skillabi-press-release-june-3-2025 **V** (the opaque score that Lyceum replaces with quotations and counts)
- Representativeness: postings over-represent business, finance, administration and management occupations by 2 to 6 points in Canada; findings "are sensitive to variation in the data gathering and information extraction of online job postings"; weight to correct. OECD, March 2024. https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2024/03/how-well-do-online-job-postings-match-national-sources-in-large-english-speaking-countries_e67205a2/c17cae09-en.pdf **P**
- Burning Glass postings over-represent management, computing and healthcare practitioners and under-represent food service, administrative support, construction and production; roughly 85% of JOLTS jobs captured in 2016. Washington State ESD. https://esd.wa.gov/media/pdf/2046/market20representation20new20uspdf/download?inline= **P**
- "only 14% of posts contain any wage information"; posted wages run 40% above administrative data in low-wage occupations. Batra, Michaud and Mongey, NBER WP 31984, December 2023. https://www.nber.org/papers/w31984 **P**
- "finds little support for a skills shortage. The evidence instead points to overeducation as a persistent, and possibly growing, feature of the U.S. labor force." Cappelli, ILR Review 68(2), 2015. https://doi.org/10.1177/0019793914564961 **P**
- Not found: a published critique aimed at curriculum alignment scores as black boxes. The representativeness and wage papers are the closest evidence, and they support "show the quotation, count honestly".

## Pitch-ready framing

Approval cycles run 12 to 18 months in the US and validation sits 12 months before delivery in the UK, reviews come every two to five years in Singapore, while a third of the skills in an average job turned over between 2021 and 2024 (Lightcast) and 39% of workers' skills change by 2030 (WEF). Every major accreditor (ABET, AACSB, AUN-QA, QAA, HESF, ESG, NAAC, MQA) already requires documented external-stakeholder evidence, and Singapore's government builds its skills frameworks from job-posting data. The gap is not the rule; it is the latency between the evidence and the classroom.
